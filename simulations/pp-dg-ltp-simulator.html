Teaching model, not a quantitative fit to published data. Mg²⁺-block relief follows the Jahr & Stevens (1990) voltage dependence; feedforward GABAA inhibition is modeled as a per-train shunt subtracted from postsynaptic depolarization before that relief is computed, with the shunt depressing across pulses within a train and a cumulative "priming" term across widely-spaced trains — a simplification standing in for the mix of presynaptic depression and postsynaptic/metaplastic mechanisms that let repeated spaced trains (e.g. 4×100 Hz/1 s, 5 min apart) overcome inhibition where a single train fails. LTP magnitude and persistence are simplified functions of the resulting accumulated NMDAR Ca²⁺ signal, gated by presynaptic drive, holding potential, GABAA strength, and NMDAR availability (APV). For TBS and 1× HFS, that deterministic signal only sets whether LTP is possible in principle; on top of it, each induction is an independent ~30% coin flip standing in for slice-to-slice variability, matching the empirical observation that only a minority of slices show lasting LTP with these single-induction protocols. The 4× spaced DG tetanus is treated as reliable, consistent with it being the standard protocol used specifically because it reliably overcomes this variability.
