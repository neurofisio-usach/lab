Teaching model, not a quantitative fit to published data. Each presynaptic spike delivers a brief Ca²⁺ transient into the terminal (rise ≈1 ms, decay = τCa); leftover Ca²⁺ from earlier spikes adds to the next one and scales release, the residual-calcium account of short-term facilitation (Katz & Miledi, 1968; reviewed by Zucker & Regehr, 2002). Release also draws down a shared vesicle pool that refills exponentially with time constant τrec, giving depression that competes with facilitation for each EPSP's amplitude — the same facilitation/depression framework as the Tsodyks–Markram model (Tsodyks & Markram, 1997; Dittman, Kreitzer & Regehr, 2000). The postsynaptic response begins ~1 ms after each presynaptic spike, the same synaptic-delay figure (Sabatini & Regehr, 1996) used for EPSC onset in the companion central-synapse simulator — the interval covers vesicle fusion, cleft diffusion and receptor binding downstream of the (essentially delay-free) presynaptic Ca²⁺ influx. Individual EPSPs are modeled as biexponential waveforms and summed linearly at the soma; τ sets how long each one lingers before the next arrives. fEPSP mode rescales and inverts the same underlying summed response to resemble an extracellular field recording — it does not model a population spike.
