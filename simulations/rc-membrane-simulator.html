The membrane as an RC circuit

The lipid bilayer is a capacitor and the ion channels are its resistor. These simulations show how the membrane charges and discharges, why capacitance tracks cell size, and how little charge it takes to change the membrane potential.

Current clamp: Injected current I first charges C (IC = C·dV/dt), then leaks through R, until Vm = Ehold + IR with τ = RC. Voltage clamp: The command voltage is stepped; a brief capacitive transient IC = C·ΔV/τr is followed by steady resistive current IR = ΔV/R. After 3τ the response is 95% settled.

Capacitance grows in proportion to area (slope +1); input resistance falls in proportion (slope −1); their product τ = Rm·Cm does not depend on area at all. A small and a large cell with the same membrane charge with the same time course, but the small one reaches a much larger voltage for the same current.
