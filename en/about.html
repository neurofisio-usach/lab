About

Dr. Patricio Rojas

Associate Professor, Facultad de Química y Biología, Universidad de Santiago de Chile

Dr. Rojas holds a degree in Biology and a PhD in Molecular and Cell Biology from Universidad de Chile, and completed postdoctoral training in the laboratories of Dr. Lawrence Salkoff (Department of Anatomy and Neurobiology) and Dr. Steven Mennerick (Department of Psychiatry) at Washington University School of Medicine, where his work centered on ion channel physiology and GABAergic inhibition.

Since establishing his lab at USACH, Dr. Rojas has led a research program built around a central idea: that chloride cotransporters and GABAergic signaling — the machinery that sets the balance between neuronal excitation and inhibition — connect circuit-level dysfunction in epilepsy to the altered excitability seen in autism spectrum disorder. Rather than treating these as unrelated conditions, the lab studies them as different behavioral and circuit-level outcomes of a common molecular vulnerability, using rodent models and hippocampal electrophysiology to trace that vulnerability from ion channels to seizures, cognition, and behavior.

This approach has also driven translational work in the lab, including the repurposing of bumetanide — a chloride cotransporter (NKCC1) inhibitor — as a candidate antiepileptic treatment.

Dr. Rojas served as Treasurer (2013–2016) and President (2016–2019) of the Chilean Society for Neuroscience, and has been a member of the Biophysical Society since 2003 and the Society for Neuroscience since 2007.

Our approach

From ion channels to behavior

  1. 01In vitro electrophysiology in hippocampal brain slices, with a focus on dentate gyrus granule cells and the axon initial segment
  2. 02Genetic and pharmacological rodent models of epilepsy and autism spectrum disorder
  3. 03EEG and behavioral phenotyping paired with circuit-level recordings
  4. 04Translational pharmacology, including repurposing of chloride cotransporter inhibitors as novel therapeutics
