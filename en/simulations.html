Teaching · Interactive tools

Simulations

A set of self-contained simulators built for our Biophysics and Neurophysiology courses. Each one recreates a classic electrophysiology experiment interactively — from the squid giant axon to long-term potentiation in the hippocampus — so that students can explore the mechanisms hands-on, with no software to install.

Eleven simulators, one common thread

From the excitable membrane to synaptic plasticity

The simulations follow the classic path of an electrophysiology course: first the generation and propagation of the action potential, then the single ion channel that makes it possible, and finally synaptic transmission — quantal, central and plastic, both short- and long-term. Each simulator runs directly in the browser.

The membrane as an RC circuit

Three tabs on the membrane as an RC circuit: charging and discharging under a current pulse, how capacitance scales with cell size, and how much charge (Q = CV) it takes to change the membrane potential.

Measuring membrane capacitance

Two ways of measuring a membrane capacitor: a classic RC circuit on the lab bench, and the whole-cell membrane test of a patch-clamp amplifier. Compare the time constant τ and the nominal capacitance with the values measured from real components.

Action potential of the squid giant axon

A reconstruction of the Hodgkin–Huxley model: voltage clamp, the membrane action potential and propagation along the axon, with interactive plots and exportable data tables.

Single-channel recording (patch clamp)

Stochastic activity of a voltage-gated Na⁺ channel (C⇌C⇌O→I scheme): single trace, multiple sweeps and ensemble average, with cursors to measure the size of the current step.

Gating currents of the K⁺ channel

A voltage-clamp simulation of K⁺ channel gating currents: oscilloscope-style traces of IK and Igating, alongside the G-V and Q-V curves. Includes recording noise and an option to average multiple sweeps of the gating current.

K⁺ conductances and repetitive firing

A Hodgkin–Huxley membrane under a sustained current pulse, firing a train of action potentials. Switch the delayed rectifier and an A-type K⁺ conductance (Connor–Stevens) on or off independently, and see their effect on spike shape, the afterhyperpolarization and the f–I relationship.

Quantal transmission at the neuromuscular junction

A replica of the Katz & Miledi / del Castillo & Katz experiments: evoked end-plate potentials, a histogram of quantal amplitudes, and a zero-calcium condition to observe failures and quantal units.

Central synapse — EPSC in voltage clamp

Postsynaptic currents at a central synapse with AMPA and NMDA components, Mg²⁺ block and low-N binomial release, on a virtual oscilloscope with realistic recording noise.

Temporal summation — Schaffer collateral → CA1 synapse

A three-channel oscilloscope — presynaptic action potential, presynaptic intracellular Ca²⁺ and postsynaptic EPSP/fEPSP — showing short-term facilitation during pulse trains. Control the number of pulses, the frequency and the postsynaptic time constant (τ).

Long-term potentiation (LTP) — Schaffer collaterals → CA1

LTP induction in stratum radiatum by tetanization, TBS or low-frequency pairing, read out as fEPSP slope or EPSC amplitude, with control over NMDA/Mg²⁺ coincidence detection.

Long-term potentiation (LTP) — perforant path → dentate gyrus

LTP at the perforant path–dentate gyrus synapse, where strong feedforward GABAergic inhibition of granule cells makes induction harder. Protocol: 4 trains of 100 pulses in 1 second, 5 minutes apart.

Use

How to use these simulations

Each simulation is a self-contained HTML file: it needs no installation or server connection, and can run directly in the browser or be downloaded for offline use. They are designed as support material for Biophysics and Neurophysiology classes, and can be used live in class or assigned as preparation or review.
