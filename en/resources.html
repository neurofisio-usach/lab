Resources

Resources for teaching and the general public

Plain-language material explaining epilepsy, autism spectrum disorder, and the science behind our research — written for a general, non-specialist audience.

We are building this section to share accessible explanations of the conditions we study, such as "What is a seizure?", "How does an epileptic seizure start?", or "What is the relationship between autism and epilepsy?". Check back soon for new material, or contact us if there is something specific you would find useful.

Note: the simulators and Neurofest materials below are currently in Spanish. English versions are being prepared.

Focal seizure simulator

An interactive simulation of a focal epileptic seizure, generated in real time from a model of coupled excitatory and inhibitory neuronal populations. Adjust the strength of inhibition and watch how seizures start and spread through different parts of the brain.

Autism simulations

Behavioral rigidity

A simulation that shows the ability — or inability — to shift attention in response to a distracting stimulus.

Open simulator

Looking at faces

A simulation showing how the workings of a neural network can explain changes in gaze patterns toward different parts of the face.

Open simulator

Auditory pulse processing

A simulation of one of the most replicated findings in autism, showing that a larger gap is needed to tell two auditory pulses apart.

Open simulator

Sensory balance

A real-time interactive simulation showing how "noise" in a neural network responding to stimuli changes as the level of inhibition is varied. This resembles what happens in the brains of autistic people during sensory overload.

Open simulator

Reliability of the response to the same stimulus

An interactive simulation showing how the response to the same stimulus can vary with the baseline activity of the circuit. Several neuroimaging studies show that autistic brains respond differently to the same stimulus.

Open simulator

Local and global connectivity

An interactive simulation of local and global information flow in neural circuits. Moving the slider favors local over global circuits. This represents the greater local connectivity in autistic people, which is thought to be related to repetitive processing and hyperfocus.

Open simulator

Seizure susceptibility

An interactive simulation showing how certain excitability parameters can increase the probability of an epileptic seizure. This may explain the higher likelihood of epileptic seizures in autistic people.

Open simulator

Measuring anxiety in an animal model of autism

Animal models of autism show more anxiety than controls, measured as the time spent in the center of an open-field arena.

Open simulator

Amygdala anxiety circuit

A simplified model of a basolateral amygdala (BLA) microcircuit, in which excitatory principal neurons interact with PV-type inhibitory interneurons.
